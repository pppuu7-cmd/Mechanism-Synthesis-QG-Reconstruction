## Node-5 common compact perturbation

Node `5` is especially clean in the displayed source ordering because every incident wedge is `(a,5)` with `a=1,...,4`. At the coincident control `g_a=1`, perturb only the integrated node by

`g_5(theta)=exp(theta J_n)`

with a compact SU(2) generator `J_n`.

For all four incident wedges,

`g_5(theta)^{-1}g_a = exp(-theta J_n)`.

By Eq. (7), the common first-order compact action is on the four **node-5 left magnetic indices** and is generated by

`-i sum_(a=1)^4 J_n^(5a)`

up to the conventional Hermitian/anti-Hermitian generator normalization. The only relevant structural fact is that the same SU(2) generator acts on all four node-5 slots.

An SU(2) intertwiner is invariant under the diagonal group action, equivalently

`sum_a J_i^(5a) i_5 = 0`, `i=x,y,z`.

Therefore the node-5 common compact one-jet is annihilated exactly by the boundary intertwiner. Unlike the boost-normal analysis of Iter076V, this cancellation uses only exact compact covariance and intertwiner invariance; it requires no Toller identity pole stripping.

## Scope firewall

This supplement does not establish a single differentiable factorized germ on a full six-dimensional neighborhood of the identity in `SL(2,C)`. The boost-normal leading singular matrix depends on the normal direction, while compact directions lie tangent to the singular SU(2) locus. Therefore separate vanishing of the six pure generator-direction first corrections does not by itself prove mixed-direction differentiability or a direction-independent full source one-jet.

It also does not establish:

- a unique global leading singular factorization of the ten-wedge source product;
- a physical nonlinear source-to-K4 curvature;
- the nominal `epsilon^-1` coefficient;
- a generic finite-spin signed P3.

The next blocker after exact confirmation is compatibility of the leading singular extraction on the normal blow-up / mixed compact-boost directions.
