## Exact combinatorial causal data remain distinct
The source defines the spinfoam edge orientations `sigma_a=±1` and wedge signs `kappa_ab=sigma_a sigma_b` at the combinatorial level. It explicitly states that this causal structure is purely combinatorial and must not be confused with the geometric Regge causal structure. The relation between the two arises only at the semiclassical level.

## Semiclassical Regge normals
For a Lorentzian non-degenerate 4-simplex with spacelike boundary tetrahedra, the source introduces five timelike 4-normals `N_a^I`. It writes

`N_a^I = s_a V_a \hat N_a^I`,

where `V_a>0`, `\hat N_a^I` is future-pointing unit timelike, and the Regge causal sign `s_a=±1` records future/past orientation of the tetrahedral 4-normal.

The semiclassical boundary spins and spinors are functions of the five normals, so they depend implicitly on the Regge causal data `s_a`.

The source assumes a Lorentzian non-degenerate Regge geometry with non-vanishing 4-volume for the large-spin analysis.

## Semiclassical bridge, not exact identification
In the asymptotic analysis the causal vertex enforces causal rigidity relating the combinatorial class from `sigma_a` to the Regge causal class from `s_a`. The source condition is at the level of wedge products/classes (`sigma_a sigma_b` versus `s_a s_b`) and saddle-point support; it does not identify `sigma_a` with a Levi-Civita orientation variable in the exact Eq.(4) definition.

## Proper-vertex orientation remark
The source explicitly notes that it would be useful to clarify the relation of its causal vertex to Engle’s proper vertex, where a constraint on the 4-volume orientation selects a single critical point. Therefore the proper-vertex 4-volume orientation is a source-motivated candidate orientation object, but the paper does **not** establish it as an exact selector inside the causal Eq.(4) amplitude.
